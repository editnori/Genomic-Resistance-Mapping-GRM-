# Genotype-to-phenotype assessment for antimicrobial resistance

Reviewed 23 October 2026. Focus: computational prediction of antimicrobial susceptibility from public BV-BRC genomes, with diagnostic and surveillance validation. This review contains no resistance engineering, new assay targets, primer designs, or sequence-level intervention recommendations.

The useful product is a species–antibiotic prediction with an explicit evidence source, uncertainty, and a route to confirmatory antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST). Whole-genome sequencing can support an inferred antibiogram, surveillance, and investigation of disagreement between genetic findings and laboratory measurements. A good retrospective classifier alone does not establish that a susceptible call can safely guide treatment.

The two preceding research attempts were reported as failed or interrupted and produced no file. Their unverified findings are not evidence for this review. The findings below come from a fresh, read-only source inspection and the cited primary publications and official resources.

## Current implementation

The inspected working tree already contains a Rust research pipeline beyond SCM and CART. Its base commit is `74c6b21f5693c48c1244d6ea78a557d37793415a`, but the Rust files were uncommitted during inspection. That commit alone cannot reproduce this implementation. Function names below identify the inspected behavior; no model fitting, remote computation, or source-code changes were performed for this review.

[src/bvbrc.rs](../../src/bvbrc.rs) defines records for laboratory AST, measurement signs and units, laboratory method/platform, standard and standard year, source/publication, genome quality, collection year, country, host, and accessions. [src/curate.rs](../../src/curate.rs) resolves antibiotic names, excludes non-drug and unresolved names, checks genome metadata, and consolidates records into one genome–drug phenotype. It stores reported calls separately from final labels and marks labels inferred from MIC with `label_from_mic`.

[src/learn/eval.rs](../../src/learn/eval.rs) names five binary learners: SCM, CART, random forest, gradient-boosted trees, and L1 logistic regression. It produces pooled out-of-fold predictions for stratified random folds and, when there are at least twice as many groups as folds, whole-lineage-group folds. SCM and CART use inner-fold selection. Forest settings are supplied by configuration; boosted trees and logistic regression use fit/validation holdouts.

In the final source read, `training_folds` constructs inner folds from the training subset, and `tuning_holdout` keeps lineage groups together when evaluating the lineage scheme. Fold validation checks for both outcome classes, and evaluation checks for missing or non-finite scores. These are source-observed safeguards; this review did not execute their tests.

The same file implements censored MIC regression and retains an exploratory helper called `conformal`. The current evaluator emits empty conformal results because an independent calibration set is not fitted. MIC evaluation constructs random stratified folds over available MIC observations and requires at least 100 observations, including 50 exact values. It is reached through the reported-binary dataset construction in [src/analysis.rs](../../src/analysis.rs), so MIC analysis is conditioned on membership in that cohort. It is not an independently assembled all-MIC cohort.

[src/learn/mod.rs](../../src/learn/mod.rs) computes confusion counts, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, F1, MCC, very major error (VME), major error (ME), and ranking metrics. The inspected evaluation does not provide a prospective test, an external institution test, calibrated probability assessment, or confidence intervals for clinical error rates. Source inspection establishes that these calculations exist; it does not establish their measured performance.

## MIC-label inference

The main curation limitation is `infer_breakpoint` in [src/curate.rs](../../src/curate.rs). It searches integer log2 MIC thresholds using consolidated records with both an MIC and a reported R/S call. It requires at least 50 pairs, at least 10 from each class, and at least 95% agreement on the same pairs used to select the threshold. These counts and the 95% cutoff are implementation choices; no independent justification or validation of them was established here.

Exact values above the threshold become R; values at or below it become S. Censored values receive a call only when their bound establishes a side. The threshold then supplies labels for MIC-only records without a reported call.

That agreement is an in-sample label-reconstruction statistic. It is neither validation of a clinical breakpoint nor validation of the genomic predictor. The threshold pools records by species and antibiotic without conditioning on standard year, method, source, infection site, or exposure. Clinical breakpoints have a different basis and can define separate S, I, and R regions. EUCAST defines I as susceptible with increased exposure; it cannot generally be treated as a synonym for resistant. [EUCAST category definitions](https://www.eucast.org/bacteria/clinical-breakpoints-and-interpretation/definition-of-s-i-and-r/), [EUCAST breakpoint interpretation](https://www.eucast.org/bacteria/development-of-clinical-breakpoints-and-ecoffs/about-clinical-breakpoints/).

Curation fits this threshold before downstream cross-validation. Thus, held-out reported labels can influence the construction of other samples' inferred labels. `Phenotype::reported_label` in `src/curate.rs` excludes conflicts and `label_from_mic` records and requires a reported R/S call, and `src/analysis.rs` builds each dataset's samples from it. Therefore, the current analysis avoids using these inferred labels as evaluation outcomes. Dataset eligibility (`--min-class`) and genome selection count the same reported calls. The inferred labels stay in the curated data, and each dataset report counts the ones it leaves out; other consumers must not silently treat them as laboratory truth.

For the primary benchmark, use independently reported laboratory phenotypes or measured MICs interpreted through an appropriate, versioned external standard. Retain inferred labels as a separate exploratory set. If empirical threshold inference is studied, fit it only within each training partition, keep inferred labels out of the primary test outcome, and compare against a reported-label-only analysis. Excluding inferred outcomes is a meaningful safeguard, but does not validate the empirical cutoff.

Several other source-level decisions need an empirical audit. `parse_call` merges nonsusceptible with R and merges intermediate, susceptible-dose dependent, and reduced susceptibility with I. `parse_mic` accepts explicit `mg/L` unless the method names disk or disc diffusion, where only an `E-test` platform counts as an MIC, because some disk-diffusion submissions label zone diameters `mg/L`. It also accepts missing units when a nonempty method does not contain disk/disc; that is not proof that the measurement is an MIC. It rounds concentrations to integer log2 steps and takes the first component of slash-separated values. Unsupported nonempty units are rejected. These are consequential assumptions for gradient-strip values, combination drugs, and inconsistent metadata.

`consolidate_mics` prefers the upper median of exact values whenever any exist, leaving censored observations out of that consolidation; otherwise it keeps the tightest bound when all censoring has the same direction. Exact MIC spread is recorded but does not itself exclude disagreement. R/S conflicts are flagged, while R/I and S/I mixtures resolve to R and S respectively.

Reported calls and MICs are consolidated separately, so a paired MIC and label need not originate from the same laboratory measurement. Preserve the original source record for each measurement before deciding which records can share a label.

## Dataset quality

BV-BRC includes laboratory phenotypes and computationally predicted phenotypes. The current fetch query explicitly requests `evidence=Laboratory Method`, which is a useful safeguard, but the returned `AmrRecord` does not preserve an evidence field. Save the query, original response, extraction date, and row-level source identifiers, and audit the source studies before treating this selection as an independently verified reference set. A database annotation and a laboratory result have different evidential roles. [BV-BRC AMR data protocol](https://www.bv-brc.org/docs/data_protocols/antimicrobial_resistance.html).

Define the measurement grain before collapsing rows: biological isolate, genome assembly/version, antibiotic or specified combination, laboratory method, measured value and bound, units, reported category, standard/version, and source. Multiple assemblies of one isolate and repeated AST entries are not independent samples. Link BioSample and assembly accessions, study identifiers, and available isolate identifiers; keep possible duplicates together across partitions. When patient or episode identity is unavailable, disclose the residual dependence rather than infer that each genome is a different patient.

The raw `AmrRecord` includes source, publication, and standard year. The consolidated `Phenotype` retains method and standard-name lists but not those source identifiers or standard years. Source-held-out validation and method-specific reconciliation therefore require linkage back to the raw records. The consolidated phenotype file alone cannot support those checks.

The primary curation audit should count original records, unique isolates, eligible measurements, missing source identifiers, contradictions, and exclusions for every species–drug task. Inspect a documented sample of accepted and rejected records against the originating publication. Keep a separate count for records excluded because a method or unit is unclear. Laboratory methods and standards may differ systematically between studies, so averaging all measurements together can hide a source effect that a model later appears to predict.

The current default genome checks reject more than 1,000 contigs, completeness below 90%, contamination above 10%, and length more than 25% from the species median, when those fields are available. Missing contig/completeness/contamination values pass those particular checks; missing genome length fails. Species names are derived from the first two words of species metadata or the genome name.

These are engineering defaults, not demonstrated clinical acceptance thresholds. Validate taxonomy and assembly suitability separately, report missing QC fields, and test whether stricter eligibility changes the composition and results of the cohort. Do not describe metadata QC as proof that every clinically relevant region was observed.

`select_genomes` preferentially downloads genomes carrying rare labels and multiple relevant labels, subject to a species cap. That can improve coverage of a research benchmark, but changes the sampled prevalence and possibly the source/lineage distribution. Preserve an unselected evaluation cohort where possible. Estimate predictive values and calibration in a target-population sample, and display the resistance prevalence beside every result. Class balancing in training is not a reason to rebalance the clinical test population.

Missing drug tests are also potentially informative: sources may test different panels or selectively investigate unusual isolates. A missing result must remain unknown, including in multi-drug models. Produce source-by-drug and year-by-drug availability summaries before deciding whether apparent multi-drug predictability reflects shared biology or the pattern of which tests were performed. This review did not inspect a frozen data snapshot, so it establishes none of the cohort sizes, missingness rates, or disagreement frequencies that this audit must measure.

## Uses beyond PCR

For a cultured clinical isolate, a validated sequence workflow could supply species identification, an inferred susceptibility profile across several drugs, and relatedness information from one dataset. Pankhurst and colleagues prospectively compared a mycobacterial sequencing workflow with routine testing across eight laboratories. This supports the feasibility of a defined clinical workflow; its results do not validate a generic BV-BRC model or remove the time required to obtain a positive culture. [Pankhurst et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26669893/).

For surveillance, public-health laboratories can combine genomic relatedness with known resistance findings and measured AST to investigate clusters, follow changes across places and years, and prioritize discordant isolates for review. Canada's CIPARS provides an operational example of using sequence-based resistance predictions for specified surveillance organisms and panels, with method/version details. [CIPARS methods and interpretation](https://health-infobase.canada.ca/cipars/antimicrobial-resistance.html?wbdisable=true).

The project's retrospective predictions could support this assessment if measured and inferred phenotypes remain distinguishable. A convenience sample of deposited genomes cannot by itself estimate the resistance prevalence of a hospital or population.

For direct-from-specimen sequencing, metagenomics offers a different diagnostic route. Charalampous and colleagues demonstrated rapid respiratory pathogen identification and detection of resistance genes. Identifying an organism or a resistance gene is a different endpoint from predicting that organism's complete susceptibility profile. A mixed specimen also requires attribution of a genetic finding to the relevant organism and clinical interpretation of colonization versus infection. A model trained on isolate assemblies needs a separate validation for this specimen type. [Charalampous et al., 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-019-0156-5).

A practical first use for this repository is retrospective assessment of where genomic predictions add information to a reference catalog and where they should defer to AST. A later clinical research use could be an advisory result reviewed by microbiology staff while routine AST continues. Autonomous treatment selection requires evidence about patients, exposure, infection site, and outcomes that public genome–phenotype pairs do not supply.

## Model comparison

Retain SCM and CART as interpretable baselines. SCM combines a small number of feature-presence/absence rules; CART makes a sequence of branching decisions. Drouin and colleagues evaluated these sparse models for genotype-to-phenotype classification. Their generalization bounds depend on statistical assumptions and do not certify a model on future institutions or divergent lineages. A selected genomic feature is predictive evidence in that dataset, not proof of a causal mechanism. [Drouin et al., 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-40561-2).

The existing L1 logistic regression tests an additive sparse relationship, random forest tests nonlinear interactions through an ensemble, and boosted trees test sequential nonlinear fitting. They should use the same eligible isolates and outer folds as SCM/CART, with training-only preprocessing and a declared tuning budget. Include a training-prevalence predictor and a majority-class predictor so discrimination and probability scores have meaningful simple comparators.

Further candidates are elastic-net logistic regression, a linear support-vector machine with separately fitted calibration, and hierarchical models that share information across drugs while retaining drug-specific outputs. These additions are proposals, not features verified in the current evaluator.

A 2024 benchmark compared four ML methods and ResFinder across 78 species–antibiotic datasets. Kover frequently led the ML methods, while ResFinder was comparatively strong for divergent genomes. Performance depended on the species, drug, and split. This is a reason to compare model families and catalogs under deployment-relevant splits rather than choose a universal winner. Its benchmark also had tool failures and unsupported catalog combinations, which should remain visible when interpreting aggregated rankings. [Hu et al., 2024](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/25/3/bbae206/7665136).

Separate two questions in the experiments. To compare algorithms, hold the feature representation fixed. To compare complete diagnostic pipelines, allow each method its supported representation but report preprocessing, supported inputs, failures, resources, and prediction time. The current Rust algorithms are implementations of model families; an RF or GBT name alone does not establish equivalence with a published package. Verify predictions and objective behavior against trusted implementations on small numerical fixtures before interpreting performance differences as scientific findings.

Neural networks, sequence embeddings, and multi-task architectures are later research comparators if simpler methods leave a reproducible deficit on locked external data. Record pretraining sources, including whether benchmark genomes could have been seen during representation learning. Added capacity does not solve biased labels or sampling.

Require any improvement to survive source/lineage shifts and include resource cost, calibration, and abstention at the same reporting rate. The same requirement applies to a hybrid that combines catalog findings with learned predictions: its combining policy must be trained and assessed without test-set tuning.

## Reference baselines

The current [src/catalog.rs](../../src/catalog.rs) reads NCBI reference sequences and uses custom k-mer containment to call acquired-gene families, followed by its own drug rules. It does not execute AMRFinderPlus and does not reproduce its complete detection or interpretation methods. Its header's approximate relationship between containment and nucleotide identity is an explanatory approximation, not an identity measurement from sequence alignment or validated assay limit. Name its output a custom catalog-containment baseline so readers can distinguish it from the reference software.

Use actual AMRFinderPlus as a gene/variant detection comparator where supported. Its documented method includes curated protein and nucleotide searches, hidden Markov models, naming rules, and resistance-associated point-mutation detection. NCBI explicitly cautions that detection of an AMR gene or mutation does not necessarily establish resistance to the corresponding antibiotic. Therefore, comparison with laboratory AST also requires a separately specified, supported species–drug interpretation policy. [NCBI AMRFinderPlus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pathogens/antimicrobial-resistance/AMRFinder/), [NCBI result interpretation](https://github.com/ncbi/amr/wiki/Interpreting-results).

ResFinder 4.0 is a complementary comparator because its publication directly evaluates translation from detected genotypes into phenotypes, including blind validation. Its reported concordance and available species/drugs must be interpreted using the paper's endpoint definitions; some comparisons concern wild-type/non-wild-type status rather than clinical S/I/R. Do not relabel those as identical clinical outcomes. [Bortolaia et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/jac/dkaa345).

For tuberculosis, include an interpretation based on an identified release of the WHO mutation catalogue when the required evidence and inputs are available. The 2023 second edition was developed from more than 52,000 isolates with paired sequencing and drug-susceptibility results from 67 countries for 13 medicines. That is evidence for a specific curated resource, not authorization to transfer its interpretation to other organisms. [WHO catalogue, second edition](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240082410).

Freeze software, database, and phenotype-rule versions independently. Record the database release date relative to each temporal test: a recent catalogue can contain knowledge obtained after historical test isolates were collected. Report that as a modern retrospective comparison, or use a historically available release for a deployment simulation. Count unsupported pairs and missing calls explicitly; neither should silently become susceptible. Evaluate common supported pairs separately from total workflow coverage. Existing panel simulations in `src/analysis.rs` are computational comparisons, not measured clinical PCR sensitivity or specificity.

## Outcomes and uncertainty

Keep binary R/S prediction as one endpoint, but preserve quantitative MIC and the original category. MIC is a measured concentration with dilution-scale resolution and sometimes only an upper or lower bound.

Nguyen and colleagues used 5,278 nontyphoidal Salmonella genomes collected over 15 years in the United States to model MICs for 15 antibiotics. They reported mean agreement of 95% within one doubling dilution and a mean VME of 2.7%. Those results show why dilution agreement and false-susceptible errors should be reported together. They belong to that collection and validation design and cannot be assigned to this repository. [Nguyen et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30333126/).

The current boosted-tree MIC objective uses squared error for exact values and a one-sided penalty when a prediction violates a censored bound. This preserves more information than treating every bound as an exact measurement, but does not estimate a full MIC probability distribution. Proposed comparators are an ordinal dilution model and an interval-censored probabilistic model. Derive clinical categories only after identifying the species, drug, testing context, and breakpoint version. Evaluate exact MIC agreement, errors near breakpoints, and censored-bound consistency separately.

An ordinal dilution model estimates probabilities across ordered MIC categories instead of assuming every value is precisely measured. Check the proportional-odds assumption if using cumulative logistic regression. An interval-censored likelihood assigns probability to the observed interval, including open-ended lower/upper limits; censored observations must not be converted to arbitrary exact values. The output can then be an MIC distribution or interval and category probabilities under a named breakpoint scheme. This is a proposed modeling design, not a claim that this repository implements or validates those likelihoods.

A direct S/I/R classifier is another comparator, but the meanings of I and dose-dependent susceptibility must remain specific to the original standard and year. An epidemiological cutoff separating wild-type from non-wild-type populations answers a surveillance question and is not interchangeable with a clinical breakpoint. Preserve original and reinterpreted categories in different fields. A multi-drug model can return several phenotype predictions for one isolate with a masked loss for unmeasured drugs; it needs per-drug errors and calibration rather than one pooled accuracy.

For MIC reporting, include exact dilution accuracy, essential agreement within one doubling dilution, mean absolute error in log2 units, directional bias, and the distribution of errors near each relevant breakpoint. In the current evaluator, `essential_agreement_all` allows a one-dilution tolerance against censored bounds. It cannot establish exact essential agreement for unknown off-scale MICs, so report it as bound-consistency under that rule.

The final source read shows that categorical MIC evaluation learns empirical thresholds within training folds and compares the resulting calls with reported binary phenotypes. It withholds the categorical summary if a required fold threshold is unavailable. This avoids scoring against labels reconstructed from a full-cohort threshold. The remaining limits are the empirical threshold's clinical meaning, random-only MIC splitting, and selection of the MIC cohort from reported R/S cases. The cohort-level `breakpoint` is still serialized as metadata alongside `fold_breakpoints`; the two have different roles.

Probability calibration asks whether events assigned a probability occur at that frequency. It differs from ranking resistant isolates ahead of susceptible isolates. Assess reliability plots, Brier score, log loss, and class-specific calibration on data unused for fitting or selecting the calibration method. Guo and colleagues studied post-hoc calibration; Ovadia and colleagues demonstrated that uncertainty quality can degrade under dataset shift. Neither paper validates calibration for this AMR cohort. [Guo et al., 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html), [Ovadia et al., 2019](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/8558cb408c1d76621371888657d2eb1d-Abstract.html).

The retained `conformal` helper splits pooled out-of-fold predictions into two stratified halves and applies class-specific thresholds in both directions. Those scores come from different fitted models; this is not a demonstrated independent calibration set for one fixed deployed predictor. Its `coverage` field measures the proportion receiving one label, not the probability that the prediction set contains the true label. Its `ambiguous` count combines both-label and empty-label sets.

The helper's VME/ME denominators include all reference-class observations, including deferred ones. These definitions would need to be visible before interpreting a low error rate as evidence for clinical reporting. The final evaluator deliberately withholds these outputs; the helper's existence must not be reported as a validated abstention system.

For a confirmatory uncertainty experiment, fit the predictor first, reserve independent calibration data, and evaluate once on a locked test set. If tuning probability calibration and conformal thresholds, use separate subsets or a prespecified valid procedure.

Conformal methods can provide prediction-set or interval coverage under their stated exchangeability assumptions; they do not automatically guarantee coverage after a source or lineage shift, or a particular error rate among singleton calls. Conformalized quantile regression is a methodological reference for MIC intervals, but adapting it to censored outcomes needs its own justification and validation. [Romano, Patterson and Candès, 2019](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/5103c3584b063c431bd1268e9b5e76fb-Paper.pdf).

Define abstention reasons before evaluation: uncertain phenotype, unsupported species–drug pair, insufficient input quality, or input outside the validated population. Report the fraction receiving a call, true-label set coverage, interval width where applicable, false-susceptible errors among called resistant isolates, and errors relative to all eligible resistant isolates. Also report the fraction of susceptible calls that are actually resistant. These denominators answer different questions. A system that defers almost every resistant isolate may have few observed false-susceptible reports and still provide little useful coverage.

## Evaluation design

The unit of comparison is an independently measured phenotype for an eligible isolate and drug, with repeated observations handled explicitly. Report performance separately for each species–drug pair, then macro-average across supported tasks. Pooled accuracy over all rows can be dominated by common drugs, susceptible isolates, or repeatedly represented lineages. Keep an identical eligible-test sample list for paired model comparisons, and also report each pipeline's ability to return a result across the complete submitted cohort.

Use the following partitioning experiments to answer different deployment questions. They are complementary, and a favorable result in one does not establish the others.

1. Random, isolate-grouped splitting measures interpolation among the sampled populations. Group duplicate assemblies, repeated measurements, and known patient/episode repeats before randomization. Use it as an internal reference result.
2. Lineage or genomic-distance holdout measures performance on genetically separated isolates. Keep each cluster in one partition, quantify closest train–test distances, and examine errors by distance. Give the biological rationale for the separation rule and report sensitivity to alternatives.
3. Study/laboratory/source holdout measures dependence on submission source, testing practices, or sequencing workflow. Keep a publication or collection together. Country holdout adds geographic evidence but does not guarantee institutional independence because sources and lineages cross borders.
4. Collection-date holdout trains on earlier isolates and tests on later isolates, using a prespecified cutoff. Use collection dates, report the time window, and keep unknown dates separate. For historical deployment claims, use only model inputs and catalogues available before the simulated prediction date.
5. External validation uses an independently collected institution, surveillance program, or prospective series that was unavailable for development decisions. Remove accession and near-duplicate overlap with training. A new download from the same repository or a random BV-BRC holdout is not sufficient evidence of external independence.

The current clusters use MinHash-derived distances and average-linkage cuts selected partly to obtain enough manageable groups. This does not prove that no close relative crosses folds, nor is it equivalent to a validated phylogeny. Likewise, database insertion dates do not establish collection dates. The selected partition must be checked against the specific independence claim it is meant to support.

Population structure can confound AMR prediction when the relationship between lineage and resistance differs between sampled collections. A primary study that explicitly varied clade-specific sampling supports testing these conditions rather than treating the random-to-lineage performance difference as a precise measurement of causal biology. [Biased sampling driven by bacterial population structure confounds machine learning prediction of antimicrobial resistance, 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3003539).

Apply nested selection within each outer training partition. Preserve its source/lineage grouping in inner validation where relevant. Keep feature screening, imputation, class weights, learned thresholds, and calibration inside the training subset. The integrated revision 3 uses training-only inner-fold construction and association screening. `training_candidates` additionally filters support and collapses duplicate presence patterns using only each outer fold's training samples; MIC fits apply the same policy within their training folds. Inner tuning shares the outer-training candidate set, so the outer evaluation remains held out while inner scores depend on that shared preprocessing.

The cached species matrix remains a feature superset: a feature eligible under a training subset's support cutoff must also pass the lower or equal species cutoff. A guard checks that relationship. Deterministic feature ordering permits a representative to be chosen without held-out presence information. The earlier revision 2 was marked invalid for this preprocessing issue; integrated revision 3 marks the corrected internal cross-validation as valid and explicitly excludes external or clinical validation. A prospective benchmark still needs a frozen development procedure and a defined mapping for later genomes.

For binary classification with R positive, report sensitivity, specificity, balanced accuracy, MCC, AUROC, average precision, and confusion counts. Give VME as false S among reference R, and ME as false R among reference S. Report negative predictive value, the fraction of susceptible calls that are reference S, at the observed test prevalence.

For S/I/R outcomes, show the full confusion matrix and identify how errors involving I are counted. Undefined metrics from absent reference classes should be unavailable; the current ratio helper returns zero for zero denominators, which must not be interpreted as evidence of no error.

Attach uncertainty to the clinically meaningful denominators. Use paired isolate-level comparisons for models tested on the same cases; resample at the patient, source, or lineage level when that is the relevant independent unit. Name the unit and confidence-interval method. A task with 25 reference-resistant isolates cannot tightly bound a rare false-susceptible error rate even if every call is correct.

Under independent Bernoulli observations, zero errors in 25 gives a one-sided 95% exact upper bound of about 11.3%; zero in 300 gives about 1.0%, using `1 - 0.05^(1/n)`. These are sample-size illustrations, not observed repository results or clinical acceptance criteria. Clustering and missing calls further change the evidence needed.

Predeclare a clinically justified error target and minimum call rate with the intended laboratory users, then plan resistant and susceptible sample counts around confidence bounds. Do not adopt 95% total accuracy, a conventional ML threshold of 0.5, or the curation rule's 95% agreement as a clinical acceptance standard. Report all prespecified model comparisons and failed tasks; use an independent confirmatory set after exploratory selection so selecting the best model from many trials does not become the reported validation.

## Clinical limits

Genomic observations can miss effects of expression, mixed populations, heteroresistance, incomplete assemblies, or mechanisms outside the reference and training data. A negative genetic finding therefore needs validated sensitivity in the intended species–drug context before it supports a susceptible report. Conventional AST also has measurement variability, especially near interpretive breakpoints. Investigate discordances through blinded review of the original measurements and identifiers and, when a collaborating laboratory's protocol permits, repeat/reference AST; do not simply redefine truth to agree with the model. [NCBI interpretation guidance](https://github.com/ncbi/amr/wiki/Interpreting-results).

Define the intended use narrowly: specimen type, culture requirement, eligible organism, supported drugs, reference method, report recipient, turnaround interval, and action on a no-call. Measure time from specimen arrival to an actionable report, including culture, transport, sequencing batches, QC failures, and confirmation. An inference runtime on an existing assembly omits much of that workflow. Track changes in the sequencing pipeline, source population, catalogue, and breakpoint standards as versioned changes requiring impact assessment.

During prospective silent validation, retain all consecutive eligible submissions in the denominator and keep experimental predictions unavailable to treating teams. Fix the model, interpretation rules, and abstention policy before enrollment. Measure reportable-result rate, false-susceptible errors and confidence bounds by drug, time to result, subgroup performance, and discordance resolution. A subsequent supervised clinical evaluation can examine how clinicians use the report, antimicrobial exposure, appropriate therapy, and patient outcomes under an approved protocol.

DECIDE-AI addresses reporting of early live clinical evaluation; TRIPOD+AI addresses transparent reporting of prediction-model development and validation. They support study documentation and do not replace clinical validation. [DECIDE-AI, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-022-01772-9), [TRIPOD+AI, 2024](https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj-2023-078378).

FDA's AST special-controls guidance concerns specified AST device systems; it is a useful official reference for identifying the appropriate evaluation framework, not automatic clearance or a directly transferable approval standard for this software. The regulatory path depends on the actual product and intended use and was not determined in this review. [FDA AST guidance](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/guidance-documents-medical-devices-and-radiation-emitting-products/antimicrobial-susceptibility-test-ast-systems-class-ii-special-controls-guidance-industry-and-fda).

## Validation milestones

1. Freeze a source and data inventory. Deliver versioned inputs, original AST records, a duplicate-isolate map, and exclusion counts. Completion requires reproducing every eligible cohort count from the frozen inputs.
2. Establish reference outcomes. Deliver a measurement audit, explicit category mappings, and separate reported/inferred-label counts. Completion requires an adjudicated sample of source records and a primary test set without empirically inferred truth labels.
3. Compare models. Deliver shared outer partitions and paired results for the five implemented learners, simple predictors, and actual reference software. Completion requires training-only preprocessing, documented tuning, verified split isolation, and visible failures or unsupported pairs.
4. Validate MIC and uncertainty. Deliver a dedicated MIC cohort, exact/bounded measurement results, independent calibration, and errors against call rate. Completion requires distinguishing interval/set coverage from reporting rate and disclosing every denominator.
5. Lock external validation. Deliver results from an independent collection with overlap checks, per-drug confidence intervals, and discordance review. Completion requires meeting prospectively specified error and reporting targets; failure leads to redevelopment and a new confirmation set.
6. Validate the workflow prospectively. Deliver a silent study of consecutive eligible specimens, including no-calls and time to usable results. Completion supports deciding whether a supervised clinical study is justified; patient benefit still requires evaluation of clinical decisions and outcomes.

These milestones are a proposed research plan. They have not been completed by this document. The present evidence supports implementation review and a defensible validation design, not a claim of clinically established genomic AST performance.

This is a focused research review rather than a systematic review or meta-analysis. Search and link checks used primary papers and official resources on 23 October 2026. Publication findings, source-observed implementation, and proposed validation methods have different evidential status throughout. No performance estimate was calculated from the repository's biological data.
